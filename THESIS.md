# Thesis: NEAR Intents volume as a leading indicator of NEAR network usage

Generated by `npm run rebuild` (`scripts/thesis.ts`) from the committed raw files on 2026-11-13. Every number below is recomputed from `data/history` and `data/raw`; nothing is typed in by hand. The same numbers drive the `/thesis` page.

## Claim

Cross-chain volume settled through NEAR Intents is a better leading indicator of real NEAR network usage than the NEAR token price. "Leading" means: changes in Intents volume this week are more strongly correlated with changes in usage one or more weeks later than changes in price are, and a model fitted on earlier data predicts later usage changes better when it uses Intents volume than when it uses price.

**Result as of 2026-11-13: partly supported. Intents volume is the stronger positive lead for 2 of 5 usage series (0 for price, 3 inconclusive), and it wins 2 out-of-sample comparisons to price's 1, with the lead on 2 of 2 contract-level series (function calls, contracts called; 52 weeks) and on 0 of 3 chain-level counts (transactions, active accounts, new accounts; 100 weeks). After the block bootstrap and Benjamini-Hochberg, Intents volume is significant on 0 of 5 series, and adding it to price lowers out-of-sample error significantly (Clark-West) on 2.**

Verdict rule: "Supported" needs Intents volume to be the stronger positive lead on three or more usage series with none for price, to win at least as many out-of-sample comparisons as price, to be significant after Benjamini-Hochberg (block bootstrap, q = 0.1) on at least three series including one chain-level count, and the nested Clark-West test to pass on at least two series. "Partly supported" needs more lead wins than price and at least as many out-of-sample wins. Anything else is "not supported".

### Pre-registered tests

Set on 2026-11-14 before any of these tests was computed; the commit that added this section comes before the code that runs them. Rules are copied from the test brief and are not changed after the results are known.

#### Part A, rigor for the headline claim

- **A1. Significance instead of a fixed noise band.** For each usage series and each predictor (Intents volume, NEAR price), the p-value of the best positive-lag correlation comes from a moving-block bootstrap (block length 4 weeks, 2,000 resamples, fixed seed), with the search over lags 1 to 8 done inside each resample so the p-value accounts for picking the best lag. Benjamini-Hochberg is applied across all series and both predictors at q = 0.10. Reported per pair: r, p, adjusted significance and a 90% bootstrap interval. The ±0.20 band stays as a secondary note.
- **A2. Does Intents add information beyond price?** Nested out-of-sample comparison with the current split (train before 2026-05-01, test after). Model 1 regresses the usage change on lagged price changes; Model 2 adds lagged Intents changes at the lag chosen on training data. Reported: test-period mean squared error of both models and the Clark-West statistic with its p-value. **Pass:** Model 2 lowers test MSE with Clark-West p < 0.10 for at least two series.
- **A3. Reverse direction.** For each series, Granger F-tests at lags 1 to 4 weeks for Intents → usage and usage → Intents, both p-values reported. If usage → Intents is significant and Intents → usage is not, the result says plainly that usage may lead Intents rather than the reverse.
- **A4. Verdict rule.** "Supported" needs A1 significance after Benjamini-Hochberg for Intents volume on at least three usage series, including one chain-level count, plus A2 passing.

#### Part B, related tests

- **B1. Intents leads app usage, not raw chain counts.** The lead-lag, significance and out-of-sample tests rerun on daily transactions excluding the top 10 receiver accounts (picked over one recent complete month) and on the top 10 only. **Pass:** Intents leads the excluding-top-10 series significantly, or clearly better than it leads total transactions. **Fail:** no lead on either series.
- **B2. Buyback coverage of issuance.** Monthly coverage ratio = holders revenue in USD divided by the USD value of the net NEAR supply increase, priced at the daily NEAR price, from the fee switch (late February 2026) onward. **Pass:** the coverage ratio has a positive trend with the slope's 90% interval above zero. **Fail:** flat or falling, or below 20% in each of the last three months.
- **B3. Where fees land, by layer.** Monthly share of total fees across L1 gas, Intents protocol revenue, Intents supply side (solvers) and app fees. **Pass:** L1 gas share falls while the Intents layers rise, with a negative 90% slope interval on the L1 gas share. **Fail:** L1 gas share stable or rising.
- **B4. Distribution concentration.** Per month, the Herfindahl-Hirschman index of referral volume, top-1 and top-5 share and the number of referrals over $1M, with unlabeled referral as its own bucket and excluded. **Pass (broadening):** the index has a negative trend and top-1 share stays under 40% in the last three months. **Fail:** the index trends up or top-1 share goes over 40%.
- **B5. Ecosystem breadth.** Weekly share of chain function calls going to a fixed group (intents.near, aurora, HOT game.hot.tg, SWEAT .sweat accounts) with the rest of the chain as the complement. **Pass (breadth growing):** the non-group share has a positive trend with a 90% slope interval above zero. **Fail:** flat or falling. A failure goes into Evidence against.
- **B6. Privacy flows.** Monthly share of 1Click volume where either side is ZEC. **Pass:** the ZEC share holds or grows after the NEAR@3.33 incentive window ends. **Fail:** it falls back below its level before 2025-10-01.

### Sample query

### NEAR Intents Explorer API

One endpoint, read page by page. The JWT comes from the `NEAR_INTENTS_JWT` environment variable and is never written to a file or a log.

```text
GET https://explorer.near-intents.org/api/v0/transactions
  ?numberOfTransactions=1000
  &direction=next                 # older rows
  &lastDepositAddress=<cursor>    # depositAddress of the last row of the previous page
  &lastDepositMemo=<cursor>       # depositMemo of that row, omitted when null
  &startTimestampUnix=<unix>      # optional window bounds
  &endTimestampUnix=<unix>
Authorization: Bearer $NEAR_INTENTS_JWT
Rate limit: 1 request / 5 s, at most 1000 rows per page
-> rows[].{depositAddress, depositMemo, status, createdAt, originAsset, destinationAsset,
           amountInUsd, amountOutUsd, recipient, referral, appFees[].{recipient, fee}}
```

| Field | Used for |
|---|---|
| `createdAt` | UTC day of the swap |
| `status` | only `SUCCESS` rows count as volume; `REFUNDED`, `FAILED` and `INCOMPLETE_DEPOSIT` give the rates |
| `amountInUsd` | swap volume, summed per day |
| `amountOutUsd` | volume on the destination side |
| `originAsset`, `destinationAsset` | routes, chains and assets |
| `referral` | volume per referring app |
| `appFees[].recipient`, `appFees[].fee` | fee recipients and app fee rates |
| `recipient` | kept only as a 16-character SHA-256 prefix, to count distinct recipients |
| `depositAddress`, `depositMemo` | row identity and the paging cursor |

Rows are aggregated per UTC day into `data/raw/intents/daily/YYYY-MM-DD.json` (`scripts/fetch/intents.ts`, `aggregateDay`). It covers 1Click swaps only.

## Data and method

- Window: 2024-12-10 to 2026-11-13 (100 ISO weeks). Intents volume comes from DefiLlama's NEAR Intents adapter (all channels, daily, since 2024-12-10). Usage comes from the NEAR BigQuery public dataset (transactions and active accounts over the whole window; function calls and distinct contracts called over the last 52 weeks, 2025-11-08 to 2026-11-13) and from NearBlocks daily charts (new accounts), series by series as in the table below. Price is NearBlocks' daily NEAR price (DefiLlama coins as a cross-check).
- All series are resampled to ISO weeks (flows summed, price and account counts as weekly totals or last values), converted to week-over-week log changes, and compared at lags of −8 to +8 weeks. A positive lag means the predictor moves first.
- Out-of-sample: a one-variable regression of usage change on the lagged predictor change is fitted on weeks before 2026-05-01 (lag picked on the training window only) and scored on weeks from 2026-05-01 onward with out-of-sample R² (against the training mean) and the share of weeks where the predicted sign was right.
- Robustness: the same tests with the largest 5% of Intents volume days capped, and again with those days removed, with receipts to `aurora` and `intents.near` removed from function calls, on daily instead of weekly data, and with the 1Click Explorer volume in place of DefiLlama's where the Explorer backfill covers it.

## Evidence for

- Function calls (BigQuery, 51 weeks): Intents volume leads by 7 weeks with r = 0.42; price leads by 2 weeks with r = 0.30.
- Contracts called (BigQuery, 51 weeks): Intents volume leads by 2 weeks with r = 0.41; price leads by 3 weeks with r = 0.21 (inside the noise band).
- Out of sample, Intents volume predicts new accounts changes after 2026-05-01 with R² = 0.02 and a 56% sign hit rate, against R² = -0.22 for price.
- Out of sample, Intents volume predicts function calls changes after 2026-05-01 with R² = 0.15 and a 59% sign hit rate, against R² = -0.36 for price.
- Robustness, largest 5% of Intents days capped: Intents volume is the stronger positive lead for 2 usage series, price for 0.
- Robustness, function calls without aurora and intents.near receipts: Intents volume is the stronger positive lead for 1 usage series, price for 0.
- Robustness, 1Click Explorer volume instead of DefiLlama: Intents volume is the stronger positive lead for 2 usage series, price for 0.
- Adding lagged Intents volume to a price-only model lowers out-of-sample error with Clark-West p < 0.1 for 2 of 5 usage series (active accounts, function calls); the rule needs at least two.

## Evidence against

- Transactions (BigQuery, 99 weeks): inconclusive. Intents volume leads by 7 weeks with r = 0.07 (inside the noise band); price leads by 2 weeks with r = 0.13 (inside the noise band). Neither clears the ±0.20 noise band with a positive lead.
- Transactions: Intents volume and usage move together in the same week (r = 0.27) more than with any lead, which reads as coincident, not leading.
- Transactions: the strongest price relationship is inverse (r = -0.23 at 5 weeks): price rises precede lower transactions, not higher.
- Active accounts (BigQuery, 99 weeks): inconclusive. Intents volume leads by 1 week with r = 0.09 (inside the noise band); price leads by 6 weeks with r = 0.18 (inside the noise band). Neither clears the ±0.20 noise band with a positive lead.
- New accounts (NearBlocks, 99 weeks): inconclusive. Intents volume leads by 3 weeks with r = 0.13 (inside the noise band); price leads by 3 weeks with r = 0.11 (inside the noise band). Neither clears the ±0.20 noise band with a positive lead.
- New accounts: Intents volume and usage move together in the same week (r = 0.23) more than with any lead, which reads as coincident, not leading.
- Out of sample, price predicts transactions changes better than Intents volume after 2026-05-01 (R² = 0.03 vs -0.04).
- Out of sample, neither predictor beats the training mean for active accounts after 2026-05-01 (Intents R² = -0.01, price R² = -0.09).
- Out of sample, neither predictor beats the training mean for contracts called after 2026-05-01 (Intents R² = -0.45, price R² = -4.44).
- Robustness, largest 5% of Intents days removed: Intents volume is the stronger positive lead for 0 usage series, price for 1.
- Transactions: Intents volume's best lead (4 weeks, r = 0.06, 90% interval -0.05 to 0.15) has a block-bootstrap p-value of 0.943, 0.943 after Benjamini-Hochberg, not significant at q = 0.1.
- Active accounts: Intents volume's best lead (1 week, r = 0.11, 90% interval -0.10 to 0.25) has a block-bootstrap p-value of 0.772, 0.857 after Benjamini-Hochberg, not significant at q = 0.1.
- New accounts: Intents volume's best lead (3 weeks, r = 0.13, 90% interval -0.07 to 0.38) has a block-bootstrap p-value of 0.621, 0.801 after Benjamini-Hochberg, not significant at q = 0.1.
- Function calls: Intents volume's best lead (7 weeks, r = 0.42, 90% interval 0.20 to 0.73) has a block-bootstrap p-value of 0.026, 0.157 after Benjamini-Hochberg, not significant at q = 0.1.
- Contracts called: Intents volume's best lead (2 weeks, r = 0.42, 90% interval -0.04 to 0.57) has a block-bootstrap p-value of 0.032, 0.157 after Benjamini-Hochberg, not significant at q = 0.1.

## What would change my mind

- If, when the daily refresh has added 12 more weeks, price's best positive-lag correlation exceeds Intents volume's by more than 0.10 on three or more of the five usage series, the claim is wrong. Conversely, the claim only becomes "supported" rather than "partly supported" once Intents volume is significant after Benjamini-Hochberg on at least three series, including one chain-level count, and adds out-of-sample information beyond price (Clark-West) on at least two.
- If Intents volume's out-of-sample R² for transactions and active accounts falls below 0 (worse than the training mean) while price's stays above 0.10, the claim is wrong.
- If removing receipts to aurora and intents.near from function calls (the "exAuroraIntents" variant) flips the winner, the apparent lead is just Intents settlement counting itself as usage.
- If the 1Click Explorer series, once it covers the full window, disagrees in sign with the DefiLlama series at the chosen lag, the result depends on which Intents measure is used and should be marked unresolved.

## Metrics that could not be computed

- The NEAR Intents Explorer API covers 1Click swaps only and starts in late May 2025; the committed backfill covers 586 complete days from 2025-03-27. Where both sources exist the Explorer reports a median 35% of DefiLlama's daily volume. Until the backfill completes, every Intents chart uses DefiLlama's series and labels the source.
- Function calls and contracts called (BigQuery `receipt_actions`) cover 52 weeks only: running `queries/daily_receipts.sql` over 2024-12-10 to 2025-11-07 would scan 549.70 GB by dry run, over the 300 GB backfill budget. For the same reason new accounts come from NearBlocks' daily charts and not from CREATE_ACCOUNT receipts. Transactions and active accounts come from BigQuery for the whole window.
- Distinct signers are an estimate of users: one person can hold many accounts, and relayers or intents hide the end user, so no "unique users" metric is claimed.
- No incentive or emissions data is available from a free source (DefiLlama's emissions endpoint is paid), so wash-like or incentive-driven volume cannot be separated from organic volume; the confounders above are the known programs.
- Weekly samples number 99; correlations smaller than about 0.20 cannot be told from noise, which is why significance now comes from the block bootstrap instead.

### Verify the evidence

Each daily refresh writes two SHA-256 fingerprints to NEAR mainnet through SocialDB (`canhav.near/canhav-atlas/anchors/<date>`): one over every committed raw file the rebuild reads, one over what `npm run rebuild` writes. The transaction for each date is listed in `data/anchors/index.json` and the per-file hashes in `data/anchors/<date>.manifest.txt`.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/wazarat/near-dashboard.git && cd near-dashboard && git checkout <commit>
npm ci
npm run verify:anchor -- <date> --rebuild
```

A match on both fingerprints proves the evidence existed on that date and that the published numbers are rebuilt from it unchanged.
